# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P005
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I had six different foster families and my belongings kept getting left behind.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody at school understood what was happening at home, so my grades dropped. I still feel behind compared to people my age.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I had the same social worker for several years and I could trust them.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I felt anxious and low all the time but the service had a long waiting list. I coped on my own and it took a toll on me.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: At eighteen I was moved into a flat on my own and nobody explained how to pay bills. That said, i was allowed to stay with my foster carer after eighteen, which made the transition easier.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker asked what I wanted before every decision.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: I had no one to turn to when things went wrong. I learnt not to rely on anyone.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I am not sure what to say about that.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would protect contact with brothers and sisters.
