# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P095
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: My placement felt stable, I knew where I would be sleeping from one month to the next.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: School was the one place that felt steady for me. Having someone who believed in me changed my attitude to learning.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I had the same social worker for several years and I could trust them. They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: That consistency really mattered to me.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I felt anxious and low all the time but the service had a long waiting list.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: Leaving care felt like falling off a cliff with no gradual handover. That said, my personal adviser helped me find a flat and set up my bills before I left.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I ran into debt because I did not understand the rules.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: When I spoke up in reviews, it felt like the adults had already made up their minds. That said, my social worker asked what I wanted before every decision.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: I had no one to turn to when things went wrong.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: We were placed together as siblings, which was a huge relief. Those relationships kept me going.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: Those relationships kept me going.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: Mentors should be available to every young person.
