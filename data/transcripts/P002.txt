# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P002
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: My placement felt stable, I knew where I would be sleeping from one month to the next.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I got extra help with my exams and it made a big difference. Having someone who believed in me changed my attitude to learning.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker seemed overloaded with cases and was always in a rush.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: It felt like I was just a case number.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody asked how I was feeling, they only asked about my behaviour. A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: My personal adviser helped me find a flat and set up my bills before I left.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: My views were written in the file but never seemed to change anything. It felt like my life was being managed, not lived.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: It felt like my life was being managed, not lived.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: The adults around me kept changing, so I never built a bond with anyone. That loneliness is hard to explain.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody helped me rebuild my relationship with my mum.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: It felt like a punishment for something that was not my fault.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: Young people should have a real say and someone to speak up for them.
