# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P006
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Every time I started to feel at home I was packed up and sent somewhere new. I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: My teacher kept pushing me and I ended up getting into college.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: Having someone who believed in me changed my attitude to learning.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: They talked to the adults and never really to me. After a while I stopped opening up to anyone.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: My carer noticed I was struggling and arranged therapy straight away. I felt listened to for the first time.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: I felt listened to for the first time.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: At eighteen I was moved into a flat on my own and nobody explained how to pay bills.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I felt completely alone for the first year.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I found out about big decisions after they had been made.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: The adults around me kept changing, so I never built a bond with anyone.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: That loneliness is hard to explain.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I was only allowed to see my brother for an hour a month in an office.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would protect contact with brothers and sisters.
