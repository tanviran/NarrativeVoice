# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P015
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Moves happened with almost no warning, sometimes on the same day. I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: It made it hard to trust that anything would last.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I missed my exams because of a move and no one arranged for me to resit them.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker seemed overloaded with cases and was always in a rush. That said, my social worker visited regularly and remembered the little things about me.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: The support ended as soon as I turned eighteen.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: I coped on my own and it took a toll on me.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I was given a flat with no furniture and nobody to call when the heating broke.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: They changed my placement plan because of what I said.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: Being heard gave me confidence.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: I had no one to turn to when things went wrong. That said, a teacher believed in me when nobody else did.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody helped me rebuild my relationship with my mum. I still grieve those lost years.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would teach us about bills and budgeting before we leave and not cut support at eighteen.
