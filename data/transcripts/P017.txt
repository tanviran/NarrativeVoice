# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P017
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Every time I started to feel at home I was packed up and sent somewhere new.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I changed schools eight times and fell behind in almost every subject. That said, my teacher kept pushing me and I ended up getting into college.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker seemed overloaded with cases and was always in a rush. It felt like I was just a case number.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: It felt like I was just a case number.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I waited six months for counselling and by then I had stopped asking for help. A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody asked me what I thought about my care plan, they just told me the outcome.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: People were paid to be there and I could tell it was just a job. That said, a youth worker stayed in my life for years and made the biggest difference.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: We were placed together as siblings, which was a huge relief. Those relationships kept me going.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would stop moving young people so often and keep siblings and friends close.
