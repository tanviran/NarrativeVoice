# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P041
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I was placed miles from my friends and my town, so I lost everyone I knew. I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: Teachers expected less from me because I was in care.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker changed every few months and I had to retell my story each time. It felt like I was just a case number.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: The support ended as soon as I turned eighteen.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I had a proper plan for leaving care and I knew what to expect.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: When I spoke up in reviews, it felt like the adults had already made up their minds.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: People were paid to be there and I could tell it was just a job. That loneliness is hard to explain.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I lost touch with my siblings because we were placed in different towns.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: I still grieve those lost years.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: Social workers need smaller caseloads so they have time for us.
