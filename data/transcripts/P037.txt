# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P037
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I was placed miles from my friends and my town, so I lost everyone I knew. That said, my placement felt stable, I knew where I would be sleeping from one month to the next.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: Teachers expected less from me because I was in care.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody talked to me about university or apprenticeships.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: They explained decisions to me in a way I could understand. They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I got counselling quickly and it helped me make sense of what had happened.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: It gave me tools I still use.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: My personal adviser helped me find a flat and set up my bills before I left.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I was invited to my reviews and my opinion was taken seriously. It showed me my opinion mattered.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: A teacher believed in me when nobody else did. She stayed when everything else changed.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: She stayed when everything else changed.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Social services supported contact with my gran and it meant a lot.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: Exams should never be missed because of a move.
