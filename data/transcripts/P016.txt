# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P016
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I had six different foster families and my belongings kept getting left behind. That said, they kept me close to my school and friends when I had to move.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I wanted to go to college but nobody helped me with the applications. I still feel behind compared to people my age.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I still feel behind compared to people my age.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: They explained decisions to me in a way I could understand. They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: That consistency really mattered to me.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I was told I was too young or too old for the service, so I was passed around.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: The support just stopped on my birthday and I had no idea where to turn. I ran into debt because I did not understand the rules.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I had an advocate who made sure my voice was heard. Being heard gave me confidence.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: Being heard gave me confidence.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: A teacher believed in me when nobody else did.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: They helped me stay in touch with my family safely.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would make counselling available straight away with no waiting list.
