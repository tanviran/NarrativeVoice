# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P007
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Moves happened with almost no warning, sometimes on the same day.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I had the same social worker for several years and I could trust them. They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: My carer noticed I was struggling and arranged therapy straight away. It gave me tools I still use.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I could not cook or budget because no one had ever taught me.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I was invited to my reviews and my opinion was taken seriously.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: Being heard gave me confidence.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: People were paid to be there and I could tell it was just a job. That said, a teacher believed in me when nobody else did.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I lost touch with my siblings because we were placed in different towns. That said, we were placed together as siblings, which was a huge relief.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would teach us about bills and budgeting before we leave and not cut support at eighteen.
