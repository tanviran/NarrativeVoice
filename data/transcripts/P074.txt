# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P074
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: They kept me close to my school and friends when I had to move. That stability made everything else easier.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: School was the one place that felt steady for me.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I am now studying and proud of it.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: They explained decisions to me in a way I could understand.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody asked how I was feeling, they only asked about my behaviour. A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: Leaving care felt like falling off a cliff with no gradual handover.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I ran into debt because I did not understand the rules.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I found out about big decisions after they had been made. That said, they changed my placement plan because of what I said.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: The adults around me kept changing, so I never built a bond with anyone.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I was only allowed to see my brother for an hour a month in an office. That said, i saw my siblings every week and that kept us close.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: It felt like a punishment for something that was not my fault.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would teach us about bills and budgeting before we leave and not cut support at eighteen.
