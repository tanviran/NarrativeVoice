# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P067
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I stayed with the same foster family for years and it gave me a real sense of home.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody at school understood what was happening at home, so my grades dropped. That said, i got extra help with my exams and it made a big difference.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody talked to me about university or apprenticeships.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I had the same social worker for several years and I could trust them. They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: They treated me like a person, not a file.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: My carer noticed I was struggling and arranged therapy straight away. I felt listened to for the first time.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I had a proper plan for leaving care and I knew what to expect.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: They kept checking in during my first months.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: They changed my placement plan because of what I said.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: My foster carer was the one steady person and I still talk to her.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Social services supported contact with my gran and it meant a lot.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: Social workers need smaller caseloads so they have time for us.
