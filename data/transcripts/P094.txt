# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P094
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: I was moved three times and I never felt settled anywhere.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody at school understood what was happening at home, so my grades dropped. Nobody talked to me about university or apprenticeships.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker seemed overloaded with cases and was always in a rush.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I was told I was too young or too old for the service, so I was passed around.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I was given a flat with no furniture and nobody to call when the heating broke.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: I was invited to my reviews and my opinion was taken seriously. It showed me my opinion mattered.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: Honestly there was nobody, I felt like I had to manage on my own. That loneliness is hard to explain.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: I lost touch with my siblings because we were placed in different towns. It felt like a punishment for something that was not my fault.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I still grieve those lost years.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would teach us about bills and budgeting before we leave and not cut support at eighteen.
