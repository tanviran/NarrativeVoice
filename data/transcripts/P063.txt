# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P063
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: They kept me close to my school and friends when I had to move.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: I still visit them now.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I hardly ever saw my social worker because they cancelled most visits.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I felt anxious and low all the time but the service had a long waiting list. That said, i got counselling quickly and it helped me make sense of what had happened.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: A crisis happened before anyone took it seriously.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: At eighteen I was moved into a flat on my own and nobody explained how to pay bills. I ran into debt because I did not understand the rules.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: Nobody asked me what I thought about my care plan, they just told me the outcome. I stopped bothering to give my opinion.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: A teacher believed in me when nobody else did.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Social services supported contact with my gran and it meant a lot.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would teach us about bills and budgeting before we leave and not cut support at eighteen.
