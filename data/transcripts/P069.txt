# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P069
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Moves happened with almost no warning, sometimes on the same day. It made it hard to trust that anything would last.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I changed schools seven times and fell behind in almost every subject.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: My social worker seemed overloaded with cases and was always in a rush. That said, she was honest with me even when the news was hard.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: I felt anxious and low all the time but the service had a long waiting list. That said, my carer noticed I was struggling and arranged therapy straight away.
INTERVIEWER: Can you say more about that?
PARTICIPANT: I coped on my own and it took a toll on me.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: Decisions about where I lived were made in meetings I was not invited to.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: A mentor from a charity checked in on me every week.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would make sure every young person is in their own review meetings.
