# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW P020
INTERVIEWER: Can you tell me about moving between homes or placements while you were in care?
PARTICIPANT: Moves happened with almost no warning, sometimes on the same day.
INTERVIEWER: How did that affect you?
PARTICIPANT: I still find it difficult to settle in one place.
INTERVIEWER: How did being in care affect your education?
PARTICIPANT: I got extra help with my exams and it made a big difference. I am now studying and proud of it.
INTERVIEWER: What was your relationship with your social worker like?
PARTICIPANT: I hardly ever saw my social worker because they cancelled most visits.
INTERVIEWER: Did you get the emotional or mental health support you needed?
PARTICIPANT: My carer noticed I was struggling and arranged therapy straight away.
INTERVIEWER: Tell me about leaving care. What was that like?
PARTICIPANT: I was allowed to stay with my foster carer after eighteen, which made the transition easier.
INTERVIEWER: How much were you involved in decisions about your life?
PARTICIPANT: My views were written in the file but never seemed to change anything. That said, i had an advocate who made sure my voice was heard.
INTERVIEWER: What was that like for you?
PARTICIPANT: I stopped bothering to give my opinion.
INTERVIEWER: Was there anyone who made a real difference to you?
PARTICIPANT: A youth worker stayed in my life for years and made the biggest difference. She stayed when everything else changed.
INTERVIEWER: What about contact with your family or siblings?
PARTICIPANT: Contact with my family was cancelled without any explanation. That said, social services supported contact with my gran and it meant a lot.
INTERVIEWER: If you could change one thing about the care system, what would it be?
PARTICIPANT: I would stop moving young people so often and keep siblings and friends close.
